Side conditions  ·  regularity, rounding, and when you may skip both

The regularity condition

Case (c) needs it, and it is the only genuinely technical part of the theorem:

∃n0, α < 1  ∀n ≥ n0 :  a · f(n/b) ≤ α f(n)

It says the children's conquer work is a constant factor smaller than the parent's — so the sum over the levels is a decaying geometric series and the root really does pay for everything.

Lemma (regularity condition). For f(n) = nβ lgγ(n) with constants β > logb(a) and γ, the regularity condition holds.

What about rounding?

For general n the subproblems are ⌊n/b⌋ and ⌈n/b⌉. In general that matters — but:

Lemma (polynomial-growth master method). If f satisfies the polynomial-growth condition, the Θ-class of the solution is the same with or without floors and ceilings.

∃n0  ∀C ≥ 1  ∃D > 1  ∀n ≥ n0  ∀t ∈ [1, C] :  1⁄D f(n) ≤ f(t n) ≤ D f(n)

Growing n by up to a constant factor C changes the function value by at most a constant factor D = D(C), for large enough n. In particular every f(n) = Θ(nβ logγ(n) log logδ(n)) qualifies — zero exponents allowed. (The lemma's local variable is written t here, to keep it apart from c = logba.)

Where the master theorem says nothing

Roura’s discrete master theorem  ·  the statement in full

Theorem (Roura’s discrete master theorem)

Let T(n) be recursively defined as

T(n) = bn 0 ≤ n < n0, f(n) + ∑d=1D ad · T(n/bd + rn,d) n ≥ n0,

where D ∈ ℕ, ad > 0, bd > 1, for d = 1, …, D are constants, functions rn,d satisfy |rn,d| = O(1) as n → ∞, and function f(n) satisfies f(n) ~ B · nα(ln n)γ for constants B > 0, α, γ.

Two things the master theorem cannot do are built in here: the subproblem sizes bd may differ from each other, and the shift rn,d absorbs floors and ceilings, so no separate rounding lemma is needed. The base-case values bn are arbitrary; they do not affect the Θ-class, though they do affect constants below the leading term.

Entering a recurrence

The Terms box holds one a b pair per subproblem size, separated by commas — the ad and bd above. Fractions are allowed. So 2 2 means 2 T(n/2), and 1 5, 1 10/7 means T(n/5) + T(7n/10) — the median-of-medians recurrence. The f(n) boxes then give α and γ; they are written in lg here and converted to ln internally, which only changes the constant B.
