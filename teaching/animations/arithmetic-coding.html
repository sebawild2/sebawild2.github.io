Arithmetic coding  ·  zooming into an interval

A stream code: instead of one codeword per character, the whole text becomes a single subinterval of [0, 1). Every character zooms into a subinterval of the current one, in proportion to its probability; at the end, the shortest dyadic interval [x/2m, (x+1)/2m) that still fits inside is written down, and its numerator x in binary is the code. All arithmetic here is exact (rationals with big integers), so the numbers agree with a hand calculation to the last digit. On the first tab every string is shown as an interval, zoomable to any depth; on the second, one text is encoded step by step. Hover a slice for its probability and its subinterval; click a slice to put that character into the text.

Input
One directive per line; # starts a comment. Probabilities may be written as decimals (0.425) or as exact fractions (1/3); they are used exactly as written.

model uniform | fixed | adaptive
alphabet 0 1 2 — the symbols, in the order in which they divide the interval. A single token of several characters is split into its characters, so alphabet 012 works too.
eof 0.15 — probability of the end-of-text symbol $, which always takes the topmost slice. eof 0 means no $ at all: the text length is assumed known, and lines 10–11 of the encoder are skipped (this is the setting of the worked trit example).
p a 0.425 — one symbol's probability, for model fixed. Symbols mentioned in a p line are added to the alphabet in order of appearance.
text 21101 — the source text. Split into characters when all symbols are single characters, on spaces otherwise.
bits 110100100 — a codeword, used in the decode direction.
n 5 — how many symbols to decode when eof 0 (without $ nothing tells the decoder when to stop).

model adaptive keeps one count per symbol, all starting at 1 and incremented after each symbol; $ keeps its fixed probability and the rest is split in proportion to the counts. At most 20 symbols of text and 12 alphabet symbols — beyond that the picture stops being one.
