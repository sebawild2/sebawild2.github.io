Peptide sequencing  ·  the turnpike problem

A mass spectrometer shatters many copies of a peptide and weighs the pieces. In the ideal case every contiguous fragment appears, so what we observe is the difference multiset ΔS of the prefix sums S of the amino-acid masses. Reading the peptide off that spectrum is the turnpike problem: reconstruct the exits of a toll road from the multiset of all pairwise tolls. The three tabs go forward (peptide → spectrum), let you solve the reverse problem by hand, run the backtracking algorithm from the lecture step by step, show the algebraic trick behind the pseudopolynomial algorithm, and finally face a real spectrum with missing and false masses.
