de Bruijn graphs  ·  gluing reads into a graph

The overlap graph puts a vertex per read and asks for a Hamiltonian path. Turn it inside out: make each read an edge between its two (k−1)-mers, and glue vertices that carry the same label. An assembly is then a walk using every edge exactly once — an Eulerian walk, and that one is easy. Nothing here compares reads letter by letter: labels are simply filed by name, and the graph assembles itself.

Small screen: this is the compact demo — the drawing, the scrubber and the commentary. The definitions panel is on the desktop version.

Lines starting with # are ignored. Of the rest, a line that is a small integer is the read length k; the other one is the genome:

GATTACAGCATGGCTAAC

Any alphabet of letters and digits works, not just ACGT — 0001110100 with k = 3 is de Bruijn's own universal-string puzzle. Leave k out and the page picks the smallest one that makes every (k−1)-mer distinct. At most 30 letters: past that the drawing stops being readable, and the point of it is lost.
