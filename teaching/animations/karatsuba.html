  The cost model  ·  what counts as one bit operation
  

    

      
Both algorithms are charged in the same currency, so that the two counters can be compared:

      
    
        
  • one bit operation per execution of the inner-loop body of long multiplication (lines 4–6),
            which gives exactly n2 for an
            n×n-bit product;
    • 
        
  • one bit operation per bit position processed by an addition or a subtraction: adding two numbers of at most
            m bits costs m+1, subtracting costs m;
    • 
        
  • one bit operation for a single 1×1-bit product;
    • 
        
  • shifts, index arithmetic and reading a single bit are free — they are addressing, not arithmetic.
    • 
      

      
This is a model, not a theorem: it is chosen so that long multiplication costs exactly
        n2, matching the slide, and so that everything Karatsuba does outside its
        recursive calls is linear in the operand width. Constant factors in the chart below depend on these choices;
        the growth rates do not.
