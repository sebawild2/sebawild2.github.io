Planar separators  ·  why √n vertices suffice

Every planar graph on n vertices can be cut into pieces of at most 2⁄3n vertices by removing only O(√n) of them (Lipton & Tarjan, 1979). The grid shows the number is right; the two lemmas show why it holds in every planar graph: breadth-first search controls the depth, and the Jordan curve theorem turns a short cycle into a cut. Click a vertex to make it the root of the search.

The argument
    Input
    First line V E, then one edge per line u v (a third token, e.g. a weight, is ignored), then exactly V lines x y giving the drawing. The drawing is the planar embedding: edges are drawn as straight segments and the inside of a cycle is computed from this picture, so the coordinates must not make edges cross. Lines starting with # are ignored.

    The same file format is used by the graph editor, so a graph prepared there can be pasted here. The first phase, the grid, is independent of the input; its side length is set above.
