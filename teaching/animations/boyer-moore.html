About this page — input format, conventions, open questions

Three stages. λ, the last-occurrence function (Θ(m+σ)); γ, the good-suffix jumps (Θ(m), built from the KMP failure links of P and of the reversed pattern); and the matching loop, which uses both and always takes the larger of the two jumps. Each stage has its own pseudocode in the panel.

Reading the matching picture. Guesses are checked right to left, so only the shaded cells that carry a letter were ever compared — everything else was skipped without being looked at. The note beside each row spells out both candidate shifts and which one wins.

Cost. Besides comparisons, the chips show how many distinct text positions were ever inspected. On English text with a pattern of length 8–10 this settles around a quarter of the text, which is why Boyer-Moore is typically faster than KMP in practice even though its worst case is worse.

About γ. γ[j] is the shift to use when the mismatch happened at P[j], i.e. when P[j+1..m) had already matched. Writing Q ~ R for "one of the two strings is a suffix of the other", the rule is γ(j) = m − max{ k < m : P[j+1..m) ~ P[0..k) }: shift as little as possible while keeping the good suffix compatible with the pattern in its new position. Both situations are covered at once — the matched suffix occurring again further left, and a prefix of the pattern hanging over the front of the matched part. Because the maximum is over all admissible k, the procedure has to keep the smallest candidate shift per j, which is what the guard does.

Stage 2 shows three rows: the value the procedure computes, the value the definition above prescribes (the two agree for every pattern), and the strong rule, which additionally demands that the character in front of the reoccurrence differs from P[j]. The strong rule never shifts less than the weak one and is what the classical worst-case analyses of Boyer-Moore use; the weak rule is simpler and equally safe.

λ is 0 for characters that do not occur in P, so the bad-character jump then moves the pattern past the offending position. The shift is never zero or negative because γ ≥ 1 always.
