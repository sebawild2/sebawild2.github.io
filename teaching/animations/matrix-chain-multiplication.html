Matrix-chain multiplication

Multiplying an a×b matrix with a b×c matrix costs a·b·c integer multiplications, so the order in which a chain M0·…·Mn−1 is evaluated changes the total dramatically. The subproblems are the ranges [i..j); the guess is the last multiplication, at some split k. Filling the table by increasing length ℓ = j − i means both values a cell needs are already there when it is reached. Hover a cell for its range; click a cell to backtrace that subproblem.

The same recurrence, computed top-down instead: recurse on ranges and cache every result. The table is now filled in the order a depth-first traversal of the subproblem DAG happens to reach the cells, and every cache hit is a subtree of the recursion that is not explored a second time. Watch the call stack on the right, and compare the finished table with the bottom-up mode — same numbers, different order.

Input
Two lines, the arrays of the lecture: r[0..n) then c[0..n), whitespace or comma separated. They must satisfy r[i+1] = c[i], or the product is not defined. Lines starting with # are ignored.

A single line of n+1 numbers is also accepted and read as the usual chain of dimensions p₀ p₁ … pₙ, meaning Mi is p[i] × p[i+1]; the two arrays are then derived and shown above. At most 10 matrices — beyond that the table stops being readable, and the point of the picture is lost.
