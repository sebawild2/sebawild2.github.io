3SAT ≤p Vertex Cover  ·  an NP-hardness reduction, step by step

A Karp reduction turns every instance of one problem into an instance of another, in polynomial time, so that the answers agree. Here a 3-CNF formula φ with n clauses becomes a graph G with a triangle per clause and an edge between every pair of complementary literals; the threshold is k = 2n. The page builds G, then walks through both directions of the correctness proof. Everything is live: click a literal to fix its variable, click a vertex to put it into or take it out of S.

Input
One clause per line, exactly three literals; a negative number is a negated variable (-3 is ¬x3; !x3, ~x3, ¬x3 work too, and ∨, |, parentheses are ignored). Lines starting with # are comments.

Verdict
G — is S a vertex cover?
G — is V \ S an independent set?
Ḡ (complement) — is V \ S a clique?
Why the same edges light up in all three pictures
An edge {u,v} of G is uncovered by S exactly when both endpoints lie in V \ S — which is exactly when it is an edge inside V \ S, i.e. a violation of independence — which is exactly a pair of V \ S that is not adjacent in the complement Ḡ, i.e. a missing clique edge. Hence
S is a vertex cover of G  ⇔  V \ S is an independent set of G  ⇔  V \ S is a clique of Ḡ,
and with |V \ S| = |V| − |S| the three decision problems reduce to each other: (G, k) ∈ VertexCover  ⇔  (G, |V|−k) ∈ IndependentSet  ⇔  (Ḡ, |V|−k) ∈ Clique. The maps (G,k) ↦ (G, |V|−k) and G ↦ Ḡ are computable in polynomial time, so all three are NP-complete once one of them is.
Input
The usual undirected-graph format: V, then E, then one edge u v per line (vertices 0 … V−1). Optionally, after the edges, exactly V lines x y with the drawing coordinates of the vertices (any scale; the picture is fitted to the box). A line S 0 2 3 presets the set S. Lines starting with # are comments. At most 30 vertices.
